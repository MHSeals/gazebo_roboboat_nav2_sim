The thruster layout, as it currently is

Four T200s at 45°, mounted at (±0.55, ±0.40) m from the centre of gravity and canted tangentially. Every motor’s forward thrust drives the boat forward, so all four forward is straight-ahead surge; yaw is a mix of forward and reverse, as it always was — just 6.3× easier, because the moment arm no longer cancels. One sign flip, and nothing given up

No motor moved and no motor was re-rated. The mounts, the 45°, the T200s and the “all four forward means forward” behaviour are all exactly as before. The only change is which way each motor is canted.

The old X pointed each motor almost radially — its thrust line sat just 9° off the line from the centre of gravity. A thrust that points nearly at the pivot barely turns anything, and in the arithmetic x sin a and y cos a nearly cancel: 0.106 m of arm out of a 0.680 m radius, 84% thrown away. Flip every cant and the thrust line runs 81° off the radius — essentially tangential, right where a force has the most leverage — and the arm becomes 0.672 m.

6.3× the turning moment, with surge and sway identical to the newton. This is not a trade — nothing was given up. The pinwheel row is the variant that orients every motor the same way round so that all four forward is pure yaw; it buys more moment still, but surge then has to reverse two motors and loses 28.6%. It was built, measured and rejected for that reason.
